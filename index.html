Design Activist Institute

Designers who resist

The Design Activist Institute is a grassroots, autonomous design collective dedicated to creating a better world.

The Institute brings horizontal, democratic decision-making and organizing to the world of design, co-creating with and as marginalized people. We are volunteer-based, cooperative, anonymous, and nonhierarchical. The Institute works with other organizers, using strategic design thinking, investigative research, and community engagement to implement design interventions that lead to lasting, palpable change.

Projects

The Institute is currently engaged in asynchronous storytelling and design projects about peace, and social, and climate justice. Get in touch if you’re interested in collaborating.

The Institute hosts workshops, events, and resources; designs posters, zines, websites, booklets, flyers, pamphlets, spatial projections, interactive pop-ups, design systems, films, and exhibitions; engages in informational insurgency and détournement; and writes and tells stories about justice in collaborative, group-directed settings. The group organizes for environmental justice, migrant rights, the rights of queer and trans people, restorative justice, feminism, racial justice, equality, fair and equal housing rights, universal health and wellbeing, workers’ rights, and against any form of hate.

Utopascope

Utopascope is a co-creative, community-led project to “design a better world.” After conversations with dreamers, workers, rebels, and thinkers about their ideal futures, the Institute produced a series of community organizing ideas as a way to return power to the people.

Utopascope explores the ways that stronger communities can build better systems for the future. It seeks new ways to manage, to spend leisure time, to work, and to imagine new common understandings so that old patterns and circumstances that keep us all down can be replaced. The project is categorized between organizing, environmental justice, people power, wellness, and economic liberation.

This project involves the community through an exchange of information and ideas, expanding into tangible, organized direct action. We invite you to propose new community organizing practices, suggest changes to existing ones, and to partner with us to create a revolutionary moment.

The visions of the worlds presented in this project are ones in which people self-organize around shared interests and needs, all people of the world have clean air and water and have access to mental and physical health services, direct democracy is embedded into daily life, and economies are liberated so that all people may have the freedom to be happy.

Studio Series

Up until the start of the pandemic, the Institute aimed to radicalize access to the design industry and design education through mutual aid, researching new methods of teaching, learning, and radical self-improvement by organizing for justice, liberation, and resistance.

The Studio Series was a free, interdisciplinary, co-creative, and autonomous laboratory. The community nominated local issues, and participants — those without formal experience in design as well as professional designers — voted to drive group-determination.

Commons-Centered Design Framework

The Institute developed a framework that combines collective design with community organizing as the highest expression of activism, named “Commons-Centered Design.” This report, titled “The Ballot and the Pixel,” explores how to improve human-centered design frameworks with organizing and activist principles, outlines organizing for direct democracy, and demonstrates how designers fit into mass movements to build collective power for the masses.

About

Inspired by the revolutionary art and design collective models of the Medu Art Ensemble, Taller de Gráfica Popular, Atelier Populaire, Luther Blissett, Guerrilla Girls, the White Rose, Gran Fury and others, the Institute strives for cooperative design solutions both self-initiated and along with other activists. These groups were born out of specific flashpoints in history, creating art that signaled defiance against tyrannical rule, oppression, and autocracy. They each manifested the spirit of critical moments, and left behind a lasting visual legacy. The Institute aspires to uphold their ethics and deep and meaningful impact.

The Design Activist Institute brings multi-disciplinary designers together with writers, researchers, engineers, activists, students, and other experts, and are always looking to collaborate. Attend a workshop, link up with other creative thinkers and community organizers who are aiming to make a difference in the world by signing up on the form above. Past speaking engagements

Scout Interventions: Disrupt Conference

April 2021

Members of the Design Activist Institute spoke at the Scout Interventions conference in a session about the Utopascope project, discussing our methods, process, and approach to remote collaboration on expansive subject matter.

Tandem Conference

October 2019

The Design Activist Institute spoke at the Tandem Conference on Collective Power and Utopian Design, leading participants through a critique of design thinking frameworks, an alternative strategy which combines democracy and community organizing, a discussion of collective power and utopian design covering an optimistic, progressive approach, and an interactive ideation exercise to discuss issues and discover workable, constructive outcomes.

Design for Different Futures

September 2019

Facilitators from the Institute led docents and curators from the Philadelphia Museum of Art in two training workshops for the Design for Different Futures exhibition. The first, an examination of different modes of utopian, creative thought about the future to prepare participants to engage in challenging conversations with the public about uncertain futures. The second, an interactive workshop on collective power, focused on labor organizing and helped participants navigate the Museum’s subsequent unionization efforts, which had yet to emerge at the time.

Designing the View-Master

March 2019

Allie DeCastro from the Design Activist Institute spoke at the Philadelphia Museum of Art on representation in design and the state of product design in a panel on Charles Harrison’s 1959 redesign of the View-Master and the ways in which we approach landscapes, people, and design today. Other panelists included Davic Hartt, Assistant Professor of Fine Arts at Penn Design, Kathleen A. Foster, the Robert L. McNeil, Jr., Senior Curator of American Art, and was moderated by Michelle Millar Fisher, The Lous C. Madeira IV Assistant Curator of European Devorative Arts.

We the Women

January 2019

Allie DeCastro from the Design Activist Institute participated in a panel discussion and closing reception at Kismet Manayunk with Morgan Plant, former Chair of Planned Parenthood PA Advocates, Andrea Ngan, co-facilitator of Design Justice Network in Philly, and Sara Atkins, Democratic candidate running in Pennsylvania’s 17th District, moderated by Maria Beatty, Kismet Community Manager.

How Might Philly?

October 2018

For the second year in a row, the Institute participated in DesignPhiladelphia, this time in a reception at Elixr for the How Might Philly? projection and web project, in which open-ended questions led to deeper explorations of constructive, real-world solutions.

Storytelling and Art for a Better World

In collaboration with Tell Me A Story

October 2017

As part of DesignPhiladelphia, Hillary Rea from Tell Me a Story led members of Design Activist Institute through a storytelling workshop, using personal narrative as a call to action for social and climate justice and sharing stories.
